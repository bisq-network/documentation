Before trading on Bisq, it\'s necessary to **fund your wallet** with bitcoin. This is because making and taking offers on Bisq requires traders to post a security deposit and pay fees with bitcoin.

Posting bitcoin [security deposits](../security-deposit/) in a 2of2 multisignature address is part of what makes trading on Bisq highly secure.

## Security Deposit Requirements {#security-deposit-requirements}

You can see the required deposits for an offer by clicking on the <code class="wiki-source-code" id="source-code-1">i</code> icon for an offer. You\'ll see a pop-up appear. On the bottom of this pop-up will be a <code class="wiki-source-code" id="source-code-2">Details</code> section with a <code class="wiki-source-code" id="source-code-3">Security Deposit</code> line.

### For Buyers {#for-buyers}

Buyers need to post a deposit of at least 15% of the trade amount, with an absolute minimum of 0.0003 BTC in Bisq 1 v1.10.11. The maximum possible deposit for a buyer is 50% of the trade amount. The offer maker sets this percentage when creating an offer.

<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="For_Sellers" aria-hidden="true">
</span>

### For Sellers {#for-sellers}

In Bisq 1 v1.10.11, the seller posts the same security-deposit percentage as the buyer, with an absolute minimum of 0.0003 BTC, in addition to 100% of the bitcoin being sold.

<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="Notes_for_Buyers_and_Sellers" aria-hidden="true">
</span>

### Notes for Buyers and Sellers {#notes-for-buyers-and-sellers}

If the size of an offer is a range, the required security deposit is calculated according to the maximum possible trade size. Example: if the buyer\'s deposit is set to 20% for an offer with a 0.05-0.10 BTC trade size, the buyer will need to post a 0.02 BTC security deposit. The seller would need to post a deposit of 0.02 BTC (for a total deposit of 0.12 BTC, since they\'d also have to post the entire *possible* trade amount).

## How to Obtain Your First Bitcoin {#how-to-obtain-your-first-bitcoin}

If you don’t already own any bitcoin, you’ll need to obtain some—ideally, as privately and securely as possible.

How much do you need? In addition to covering a security deposit (as described above), traders also need to cover mining fees and [trading fees](../trading-fees/). Estimates for both are shown in the interface when making or taking an offer. The earlier 0.002 BTC estimate is not a universal funding requirement: use the deposit, trading fee and mining fee shown for your chosen offer.

Here are some ideas for how to do that.

<figure class="wiki-source-image" id="source-image-2"><a href="../../media/wiki/3e147b0a1c06f39791ed7aee92f729c100b22e340abe11f3f4a1fad12aa5dcf6.png"><img alt="A graphical overview of how to get your first bitcoin privately." decoding="async" height="1130" loading="lazy" src="../../media/wiki/3e147b0a1c06f39791ed7aee92f729c100b22e340abe11f3f4a1fad12aa5dcf6.png" width="450"></a><figcaption>A graphical overview of how to get your first bitcoin privately.<br><small>Historical thumbnail; original resolution not recovered. <a href="../../recovered-media/">Provenance catalogue</a>.</small></figcaption></figure>
<ul>
<li><strong><a href="../bisq-easy/">Bisq Easy</a></strong>. Bisq Easy is a trade protocol for Bisq 2 that allows users to buy bitcoin with no trade fees and no security deposit. Bisq Easy is a social trade protocol. It is meant for bitcoin buyers with limited experience using peer to peer software to purchase no KYC bitcoin and to be smoothly on-boarded by experienced and helpful bitcoin sellers.</li>
<li><a href="../getting-your-first-btc/"><p><strong>Matrix room</strong></p>
</a>. Bisq's Matrix chat offers a <code class="wiki-source-code" id="source-code-4">Get your first BTC</code> room for trusted and bonded Bisq users to sell first-time Bisq users their first BTC so they can use Bisq.</li>
<li><strong>Friends and family</strong>. Simply buy a small amount of bitcoin directly from someone you trust.</li>
<li><strong>Bitcoin ATM</strong>. Some bitcoin ATMs don't require ID for smaller purchases, and some don't require any ID at all. You can find bitcoin ATMs in your area on <a href="https://coinatmradar.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer">Coin ATM Radar</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Sell something</strong>. Do you offer a product or service? Use BTCPayServer to collect BTC for it! Otherwise don't forget about local marketplaces like Craigslist or Facebook Marketplace—many people are willing to buy and sell everyday goods for BTC.</li>
<li><strong>Work for it</strong>. While sites to find gigs that pay in bitcoin never really panned out, it may not hurt to let a client or business contact know that you also accept bitcoin for payment.</li>
<li><strong>Meetups</strong>. People at bitcoin meetups might have bitcoin. Crazy, right? Find out where and when they take place on Meetup, Twitter, and other places where people talk about bitcoin.</li>
<li><strong>Vouchers</strong>. Companies like <a href="https://fastbitcoins.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer">Fastbitcoins.com</a> and <a href="https://azte.co/" rel="noopener noreferrer">Azte.co</a> may sell vouchers at physical stores in your area for cash. There aren't a whole lot of locations yet, but more are being added all the time.</li>
</ul>

It\'s highly recommended that you avoid using a corporate, centralized exchange to get your first bitcoin. Most such exchanges (like Coinbase, Binance, Kraken, etc) require a significant amount of personal information, putting you at risk by tying your identity to your bitcoin (as well as by storing all this information in databases with that of others, forming what are commonly referred to as \"honeypots\"—highly attractive targets for hackers). Also, since bitcoin transactions are public and easily traceable, any future transactions involving bitcoin purchased with these services can be traced back to you.

Bisq is built from the ground up to avoid this privacy fiasco, so we strongly recommend avoiding centralized exchanges.

## Sending Bitcoin to your Bisq wallet {#sending-bitcoin-to-your-bisq-wallet}

Once you’ve gotten enough bitcoin for your security deposit and fees, you can move it into Bisq’s built-in wallet. This isn’t strictly required, but it’ll make trading more convenient since the funds you need to accept an offer will be readily available when you need them.

To send bitcoin to your Bisq wallet, you\'ll need an address. You\'ll find one on <code class="wiki-source-code" id="source-code-5">Funds</code> > <code class="wiki-source-code" id="source-code-6">Receive Funds</code>.

You might see many addresses listed. Pick the address on the bottom next to the QR code.
