Sometimes Bisq trades don\'t go smoothly, and **dispute resolution** is required to determine an outcome.

Dispute resolution on Bisq has three formal levels: trader chat, [medition](../mediation/), and [arbitration](../arbitration/).

Most issues on Bisq are minor and easily resolved when traders communicate with each other. [Mediation](../mediation/) is intended to resolve the vast majority of remaining issues. [Arbitration](../arbitration/) is a rare last resort measure for extreme scenarios.

<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="Level_1:_Trader_chat" aria-hidden="true">
</span>

## Level 1: Trader chat {#level-1-trader-chat}

If you\'re having any issues completing a trade, or if you just want to keep your counterparty informed (e.g., in case you\'re delayed completing your end of the deal), you can send your counterparty a message through Bisq\'s end-to-end encrypted chat mechanism. This allows traders to communicate with each other throughout the course of a trade to resolve potential trade issues quickly without involving a third party like a [mediator](../mediator/) or [arbitrator](../arbitrator/).

### Trader chat rules {#trader-chat-rules}

Hopefully, by following these rules you can work directly with your trading peer to solve issues quickly and amicably.

<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="Good_Communication_is_Key" aria-hidden="true">
</span>

#### Good Communication is Key {#good-communication-is-key}

Good communication between traders can resolve most disputes. It\'s recommended for traders to communicate any issues they\'re having to keep their peer informed.

However, **responding to chat messages is always optional**. If you’re not comfortable responding, don’t. You have no obligation to communicate over this medium with traders. You must, however, respond to [mediators](../mediator/) and [arbitrators](../arbitrator/) when needed.

<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="Respect_a_Peer’s_Decision_to_Avoid_Responding" aria-hidden="true">
</span>

#### Respect a Peer’s Decision to Avoid Responding {#respect-a-peers-decision-to-avoid-responding}

If a peer seems unresponsive or has signaled their intention to refrain from chatting, please respect it and don’t pressure them.

<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="Do_Not_Send_Links_of_Any_Kind" aria-hidden="true">
</span>

#### Do Not Send Links of Any Kind {#do-not-send-links-of-any-kind}

For safety reasons, sending links is prohibited. If you want to share a link, describe it instead.

*Not allowed:*

<p><code><code class="wiki-source-code" id="source-code-1">https://blockstream.info/tx/4b5417ec5ab6112bedf539c3b4f5a806ed539542d8b717e1c4470aa3180edce5</code></code></p>

*Allowed:*

`Hey, could you look up txid 4b5417ec5ab6112bedf539c3b4f5a806ed539542d8b717e1c4470aa3180edce5 in your favorite block explorer?`

<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="Do_Not_Encourage_Trading_Away_from_Bisq" aria-hidden="true">
</span>

#### Do Not Encourage Trading Away from Bisq {#do-not-encourage-trading-away-from-bisq}

Trades away from Bisq lack Bisq’s security mechanisms. If something goes wrong with an off-Bisq trade, you’re on your own.

<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="Do_Not_Share_Sensitive_Information" aria-hidden="true">
</span>

#### Do Not Share Sensitive Information {#do-not-share-sensitive-information}

Such information is **never** wise to share in general and is never needed to solve trade disputes on Bisq. This includes passwords, seed words and private keys.

<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="Do_Not_Engage_in_Foul_Play" aria-hidden="true">
</span>

#### Do Not Engage in Foul Play {#do-not-engage-in-foul-play}

Any attempts to exploit counterparties with social engineering scams, etc., are prohibited.

<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="Keep_Conversation_Scope_Limited_to_the_Trade" aria-hidden="true">
</span>

#### Keep Conversation Scope Limited to the Trade {#keep-conversation-scope-limited-to-the-trade}

Please don’t use Bisq’s trader chat as a general-purpose messenger. Unnecessary exchanges add unnecessary strain to Bisq’s peer-to-peer network.

<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="Keep_Conversation_Friendly_and_Respectful" aria-hidden="true">
</span>

#### Keep Conversation Friendly and Respectful {#keep-conversation-friendly-and-respectful}

Because friendlier traders resolve disputes quicker, and no one wants to deal with a jerk.

## Level 2: Mediation {#level-2-mediation}

If issues persist after discussing them with the counterparty, you may need to involve a [mediator](../mediator/).

**[Mediation](../mediation/) can be initiated at any point during or after the trade period. To do this, select the trade from the \'Open Trades\' list and press \'Ctrl\' + \'O\'.**

### How Mediation Works {#how-mediation-works}

[Mediators](../mediator/) assess a trade situation between two users and propose a payout suggestion. They don\'t hold a key in the multisig escrow, so their proposal isn\'t authoritative.

<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="Trading_Peer_Expectations" aria-hidden="true">
</span>

#### Trading Peer Expectations {#trading-peer-expectations}

- Cooperate with the [mediator](../mediator/), providing requested information (quality proof will be asked for payments or bank issues)
- Respond to messages within 48 hours. Non-response might result in [penalties](../table-of-penalties/).

<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="Mediator_Response_Time" aria-hidden="true">
</span>

#### Mediator Response Time {#mediator-response-time}

[Mediators](../mediator/) may take up to 48 hours to respond. **Please be patient**.

<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="Payout_Suggestion" aria-hidden="true">
</span>

#### Payout Suggestion {#payout-suggestion}

- If both peers agree, the payout is completed, and the trade is closed.
- If one or both disagree, a peer can engage an [arbitrator](../arbitrator/) to reassess the situation (see [arbitration](../dispute-resolution-in-bisq-1/#level-3-arbitration) below).

<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="When_are_you_eligible_for_a_penalty?" aria-hidden="true">
</span>

### When are you eligible for a penalty? {#when-are-you-eligible-for-a-penalty}

If a trader does not follow the [trading rules](../trading-rules/), they risk being [penalized](../table-of-penalties/). Any [penalties](../table-of-penalties/) will be taken from the trader\'s BTC [security deposit](../security-deposit/).

The trader at fault will be charged the [penalty](../table-of-penalties/), and their [security deposit](../security-deposit/) will be deducted a BTC amount.

The trader that is wronged will receive the [BTC penalty](../table-of-penalties/) as compensation. The payout transaction for the trade will include funds from the other trader\'s [security deposit](../security-deposit/).

For more information about what to expect, you can see the [table of penalties](../table-of-penalties/). This table acts as a guideline for traders, [mediators](../mediator/), and [arbitrators](../arbitrator/) to ensure [penalty](../table-of-penalties/) are applied in a fair and impartial way.

<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="Who_are_mediators?" aria-hidden="true">
</span>

### Who are mediators? {#who-are-mediators}

[Mediator](../mediator/) roles are [bonded roles](https://docs.bisq.network/user-dao-intro.html#ensure-honesty-in-high-trust-roles) in the [Bisq DAO](../dao-user-reference/#bisq-dao). Anyone can propose to become a [mediator](../mediator/), but approval will depend on the candidate\'s familiarity with Bisq [trading rules](../trading-rules/) and the network’s need for more [mediators](../mediator/) at the time a proposal is made.

Once a proposal to become a [mediator](../mediator/) is approved by [DAO voting](../dao-user-reference/#voting-cycle), the [mediator](../mediator/) must lock a 10,000 BSQ bond to become active. This bond helps to ensure their continued availability and performance in the role, and can be confiscated by [DAO voting](../dao-user-reference/#voting-cycle) in extreme circumstances (negligence, foul play, prolonged absence, etc).

You can see more details (current role owners, regular updates, etc) on the [mediator role issue](https://github.com/bisq-network/roles/issues/83).

<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="Where_to_communicate_with_mediators?" aria-hidden="true">
</span>

### Where to communicate with mediators? {#where-to-communicate-with-mediators}

You can communicate with your [mediator](../mediator/) directly in the Bisq app. When you initiate a trade and open a [mediation](../mediation/), a support ticket is automatically created within the app. You can find this in the \'Support\' > \'Mediation\' section.

Communicating with [mediators](../mediator/) inside the app is preferred.

In case you\'re unable to communicate within the Bisq app for any reason, you can also reach out to them on [Matrix](../matrix-bisq-chat/).

Find more information on [finding your mediator here](../finding-your-mediator/).

<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="How_to_find_your_mediators?" aria-hidden="true">
</span>

### How to find your mediators? {#how-to-find-your-mediators}

Your [mediator\'s](../mediator/) name should be visible when you open a mediation ticket. Look for it under the \'support agent\' column.

If, however, you have a trade not in mediation but would like to know your assigned mediator\'s name, please refer to [Finding your mediator](../finding-your-mediator/).

## Level 3: Arbitration {#level-3-arbitration}

<figure class="wiki-source-image" id="source-image-3"><a href="../../media/wiki/2caebcdeac6af275a59fadaec67e8a89bcb055e1df579d7a1098b6ecafff5693.png"><img alt="Reject mediator's suggestion in order to start arbitration." decoding="async" height="956" loading="lazy" src="../../media/wiki/2caebcdeac6af275a59fadaec67e8a89bcb055e1df579d7a1098b6ecafff5693.png" width="1750"></a><figcaption>Reject mediator's suggestion in order to start arbitration.<br><small>Historical full-size recovered source. <a href="../../recovered-media/">Provenance catalogue</a>.</small></figcaption></figure>

[Arbitration](../arbitration/) is the final tier of dispute resolution on Bisq. It\'s designed to be rare, and its measures are intended for extreme situations. [Arbitration](../arbitration/) is only available when:

- One or both traders reject a [mediator\'s](../mediator/) proposed solution.
- The <a href="../arbitration/">time-locked transaction</a> <small class="wiki-link-gap">[section not recovered: Time-Locked Payout Transaction]</small> (the delayed payout transaction created at the trade\'s start) is published by one of the traders.

This transaction sends **all** funds in the multisig escrow (both traders\' funds) to the [Burning Men](../burning-men/).

The delayed payout transaction can only be published 10 days after the deposit transaction is confirmed for altcoin trades, or 20 days for fiat trades.

Consider this transaction as a safeguard or fallback option, to be used only when other options have failed.

<aside class="wiki-admonition" role="note">

**Warning**

[Mediation](../mediation/) is expected to be the best resolution for both traders. If a trade goes to [arbitration](../arbitration/), the [arbitrator](../arbitrator/) will pay out the trade amount plus one peer\'s [security deposit](../security-deposit/). This means the total [arbitration](../arbitration/) payout will be less than the [mediation](../mediation/) payout. Both traders agreeing to the [mediator\'s](../mediator/) suggestion is the preferred outcome. Requesting [arbitration](../arbitration/) should be reserved for exceptional circumstances, such as one peer not responding, or if a trader believes the [mediator](../mediator/) did not make a fair payout suggestion.

This dynamic enables a form of mutually assured destruction, driving dispute resolution on Bisq without relying on trusted third parties.

</aside>

Practically, here\'s how [arbitration](../arbitration/) works:

<ol>
<li>If you're trading with an unresponsive peer or feel the <a href="../mediator/">mediator's</a> suggestion was unfair, publish the time-locked transaction as soon as possible and request <a href="../arbitration/">arbitration</a>.</li>
<li>Collaborate with the <a href="../arbitrator/">arbitrator</a> to clarify your case's details.</li>
<li>If the <a href="../arbitrator/">arbitrator</a> sides with you, they will personally reimburse you.</li>
<li>The <a href="../arbitrator/">arbitrator</a> will then request reimbursement from the <a href="../dao-user-reference/#bisq-dao">Bisq DAO</a> for the reimbursements they've paid. This isn't something you need to worry about as a trader, but it's good to know how the process works on both sides.</li>
<li><a href="../arbitrator/">Arbitrators</a> are required to respond to messages within 5 days, so it may take a bit longer for them to respond than mediators (you should still respond to messages within 2 days).</li>
</ol>

## If all else fails {#if-all-else-fails}

One advantage of a flat, decentralized governance structure that encourages user involvement is that no user should ever feel hindered from receiving a fair trade outcome because of a single individual\'s decision. Bisq isn\'t a black-box corporation with inscrutable processes. It\'s an open software project that is governed openly.

Therefore, for whatever reason, if the process above doesn\'t work for you, you can make a [make a reimbursement request](../making-a-reimbursement-request/) yourself in the [Bisq DAO](../dao-user-reference/#bisq-dao). The [Bisq DAO](../dao-user-reference/#bisq-dao) **not any single individual is the ultimate arbiter of policy and decision-making for all things Bisq**.

In fact, this is how [arbitration](../arbitration/) will eventually be handled anyway.
