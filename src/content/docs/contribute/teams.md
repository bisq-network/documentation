## Teams

Bisq coordinates work across contributors and role owners rather than a single employer. The recovered structure grouped software development, infrastructure operations, user support, security and outreach. Teams and membership can change; check the [Bisq GitHub organization](https://github.com/bisq-network) and current role issues before relying on a roster.

The historical diagram named twelve areas: operations, development, support, growth, security, infrastructure, product, UX, projects, decentralization, defense and finance. Its proposed twelve-team limit and 90% in-team-coordination goal were organizational ideas, not verified current policy. It welcomed pseudonymous contributors and described work being reviewed through compensation requests. Use the current project rather than this snapshot to find an active team.

## Development team

The recovered development page linked software maintainers, a developer chat, the critical-bug process and development workflow. Current contribution instructions are in the owning repository: [Bisq 1](https://github.com/bisq-network/bisq) and [Bisq 2](https://github.com/bisq-network/bisq2). Contact maintainers before substantial work and note the current pause on new compensated development contributors.

## Operations team

The historical operations team coordinated availability, privacy and resilience of seed nodes, price nodes, explorers, websites and other services. Individual operators had distinct duties. The [operations repository](https://github.com/bisq-network/ops) and current [roles issues](https://github.com/bisq-network/roles/issues) are better starting points than the old team roster. See [infrastructure](../infrastructure/).

## Support team

The recovered support team assisted users, maintained support material and coordinated mediation, arbitration and refund-related work. Public support and private team channels have different purposes; do not put payment details or logs in a public issue. The historical source lists the [support repository](https://github.com/bisq-network/support). See [support work](../support/) and [getting help with Bisq 1](../../bisq-1/dispute-help/).

## Security team

The recovered page describes threat analysis, security review, prioritization, disclosure coordination and security improvements. It marks the former Security Team Lead role as retired. For a vulnerability, follow the affected repository's current security policy rather than posting exploit details publicly or assuming an old team roster.

## Growth team

The recovered Growth Team page explicitly marks the team retired. It recorded community outreach, website and translation work, and occasional market-making bounties, with prior agreement on scope and funding. These are historical responsibilities, not evidence that the former team, budget or bounty program is active. The current [contribution policy](https://bisq.network/blog/open-source-development-in-the-age-of-ai/) and project maintainers govern present participation.
