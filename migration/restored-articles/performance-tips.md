<div class="wiki-recovered-article" lang="en"><p>Bisq is designed and tested to work well on most user environments. This includes all major operating systems (Linux, macOS, Windows), as well as some more exotic ones (<a href="../running-bisq-on-qubes/">Qubes</a>, <a href="../running-bisq-on-tails/">Tails</a>).</p>&#10;<p>However, in certain setups and in certain situations, Bisq may be slower than usual.</p>&#10;<p>If that is the case for you, here are a few performance tips for you to consider.</p>&#10;<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="General_Tips" aria-hidden="true"></span><h2 id="general-tips"> General Tips </h2>&#10;<ul>&#10;<li>Use the latest Bisq version</li>&#10;<li>Expect a somewhat slower UI immediately upon opening Bisq, as long as Bisq is "Synchronizing DAO"&#10;<ul>&#10;<li>You can check if "Synchronizing DAO" is running in the bottom bar of the UI. If it is running, it will be shown as a progress bar.</li>&#10;<li>Depending on the user's environment and hardware setup, this process <em>may</em> be resource intensive (high CPU usage, sluggish UI)</li>&#10;<li>After this process is complete, Bisq should be faster and more responsive</li>&#10;</ul></li>&#10;</ul>&#10;<span class="wiki-legacy-anchor" id="macOS-specific_Tips" aria-hidden="true"></span><h2 id="macos-specific-tips"> macOS-specific Tips </h2>&#10;<p>Avoid minimizing the Bisq app. In certain situations, macOS may try to allocate less resources to it (CPU, RAM), de-prioritize its threads, de-prioritize or interrupt open network connections, or attempt other optimizations to free up system resources.</p>&#10;</div>
