## Contributor checklist

Begin by using the software, reading the relevant documentation and finding the project that owns the issue. Join the appropriate [Bisq community channel](https://bisq.chat), explain what you want to improve, and ask whether maintainers can review it. Start with a bounded result, identify a reviewer, and agree on scope before investing substantial time. Keep the work, evidence and follow-up linked to an issue or pull request. See [contributing](../getting-started/) for the present pause on new compensated development contributors.

The recovered checklist also emphasized learning the project, choosing a consistent pseudonym, communicating asynchronously, documenting work for review and maintaining what you deliver. Its specific old teams, meetings, payment expectations and tools should be confirmed with current maintainers.

## Development process

Follow the contribution instructions in the repository you are changing. For Bisq 1, the [v1.10.11 contribution guide](https://github.com/bisq-network/bisq/blob/ea9d5dff90c99ac462f2b0a7bf9ef92683956619/CONTRIBUTING.md) describes focused changes and signed commits; Bisq 2 has its own [repository instructions](https://github.com/bisq-network/bisq2/blob/cfe8ab251f2b6579f29e9955036afc33ba36861e/README.md). Record why the change is needed, what it changes, how it was tested and any known gaps. Do not treat AI-generated output as reviewed merely because it reads well.

## Critical bugs

The recovered process defines a critical bug as one that blocks a core use case, repeatedly sends users to support, or risks funds or privacy. For a suspected vulnerability use the affected repository's security policy; avoid public disclosure of exploit details. For other problems, provide a minimal reproduction, affected version and observable impact without exposing user secrets. A maintainer can triage and prioritize it.

Historically, maintainers used an `is:critical` label and a board with to-do and in-progress states. Contributors asked for assignment, gave progress updates, and documented when work stopped. A fix merged or an investigation blocked by an external dependency were examples of delivered work in the old compensation process, not guarantees of a present payment or current board workflow.

## Project management

The recovered project process separated a multi-person effort into proposal, triage, stakeholder feedback, approval, budgeting, work, review and closure. An owner was accountable for a clear delivery result. Approval and budget were separate decisions; contributors were cautioned not to assume cooperation or compensation before both. Work could be blocked, paused or resumed, and a project could close as delivered, aborted, deferred or superseded. The old labels, boards and biweekly call cadence are historical examples, not an assertion that those tools and meetings remain active.

## Standup

A concise update can state what was completed, what is next and what is blocked. The recovered page was an index to the former standup process rather than a detailed current policy; agree reporting frequency and channel with the team instead of assuming an old schedule.
